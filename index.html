Modelling Respiratory Failure in Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis: Physiology, Mathematical Model, and Validation Against Published Cases

Bill Truschel

Read / download the article (PDF)

Abstract

Respiratory failure is the main cause of death in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and non-invasive ventilation (NIV), particularly when started early, prolongs survival. This article explains the respiratory physiology of ALS and a mathematical model of it implemented in the VentiVerse ventilatory physiology simulator. Clinic measurements (MIP, MEP, FVC % predicted, blood gases, demographics) set the respiratory muscle capacity, which is limited by length–tension, force–velocity and fatigue; the model also includes effort-driven rapid shallow breathing, ALS hypermetabolism, a bicarbonate-dependent CO₂ setpoint, sleep stages, and the NIV interface.

Bulbar- and limb-onset presets are demonstrated across wake and sleep, and simulations are compared one by one with published ALS case reports, with a discussion of where the model agrees and why it may differ. A dedicated section uses the cases and simulations to develop mechanical ventilation strategies: why support is needed most during sleep, pressure-targeted versus volume-assured modes, backup rate and ventilator capture, inspiratory time, EPAP and interface, daytime and mouthpiece ventilation, and a simulation-based titration workflow.
